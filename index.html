    How Belly Check works

    Where cars scrape

    The front bumper catches in dips, where the ramp gets steeper (approach angle). The underbody catches on crests, where it flattens out again (breakover angle). The rear bumper catches where the ramp levels out behind the car (departure angle). How sharply the slope changes matters more than the average slope.

    Measuring your ramp

    Split the ramp wherever the slope changes. For each piece, measure the length with a tape and the angle with a phone level app, or the rise with a straight edge and a spirit level. Short easing pieces at the top and bottom make a big difference, so include them.

    Reading the result

    Green clears with more than your safety margin, amber clears with less, red touches. A tight result is worth a real check: measure your own bumpers and underbody, and allow for passengers and luggage.
